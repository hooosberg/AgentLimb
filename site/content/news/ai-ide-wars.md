The AI IDE market has exploded. Cursor, Windsurf (by Codeium), and Trae (by ByteDance) are leading a wave of AI-first code editors that go far beyond autocomplete.

## The Competitors

**Cursor** pioneered the AI-first IDE concept. Built on VS Code, it offers multi-file editing, codebase-aware suggestions, and agentic task completion.

**Windsurf** differentiates with its "Cascade" feature — a step-by-step agent that can plan and execute complex refactors across multiple files.

**Trae** brings ByteDance's engineering muscle. Free tier is generous, and the agent capabilities are rapidly improving.

**Kiro** (AWS) takes a spec-driven approach — write a spec, and the AI implements it.

## AgentLimb's Position

AgentLimb isn't an IDE — it's a browser arm. All of these IDEs can use AgentLimb to extend their capabilities into the browser. Write code in Cursor, test it in your browser with AgentLimb, post about it on social media — all in one workflow.

## The Takeaway

The IDE wars are good for developers. More competition means better tools, faster innovation, and — with MCP standardization — the freedom to switch between them without losing your setup.
